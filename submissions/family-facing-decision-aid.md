# Questions and preferences for the next appointment

**A draft discussion guide, 23 October 2026. Not a validated patient decision aid.**

This guide has not been field-tested, peer reviewed or endorsed by a clinical team. It draws on decision-aid principles but does not claim IPDAS certification or full compliance. Read it with the [plain-language research summary](family-facing-summary.md).

## 1. Agreeing a surveillance plan

Published SIOP-Europe guidance recommends renal ultrasound every three months until age seven for MVA (PMID 34134020). Older schedules and no scheduled surveillance are not presented here as equivalent alternatives. Any adaptation should be discussed with the treating team, including the child's circumstances, expected benefit, burdens and uncertainty.

Questions to bring:

- Which guidance is our plan based on, and when will it be reviewed?
- Which scan is needed, how often, and until what age?
- What should we do about a missed appointment or symptoms between scans?
- What happens if a scan finds something uncertain?
- Can visits be combined, arranged nearer home or supported with transport?

**The burden is real.** Four scans a year for seven years is approximately 28 scheduled scans, depending on when surveillance starts and how boundary dates are counted. This is appointment arithmetic, not a prediction of this child's actual care. Travel, time off work, waiting for results and the child's experience matter too. **[OUR MEASUREMENT]**

**The uncertainty is also real.** We do not have a reliable MVA-specific estimate of the chance that screening will improve outcome or produce a false alarm. A study of 63 children with Beckwith–Wiedemann syndrome reported ultrasound abnormalities in 25; that is an abnormality-detection rate in another condition, not a 40% false-positive rate for MVA. Observational BWS work associates screening with earlier-stage detection but does not remove confounding or prove the same benefit in MVA. PMID 19610116; PMID 31583434.

## 2. Exploring reproductive options, if and when wanted

Ask a genetics team first to clarify the variants, phase and recurrence risk. Under a confirmed recessive model in which each parent carries one relevant allele, a one-in-four affected-pregnancy probability is the usual model; our unphased analysis alone does not establish that premise. **[OUR INFERENCE]**

Depending on clinical circumstances and personal preferences, discussion may include IVF with PGT-M, testing during a pregnancy, conception without genetic testing, donor gametes, adoption or deciding not to pursue another pregnancy. These options have different medical, practical, emotional and financial implications. This guide does not establish availability or recommend one.

For a UK discussion, the dated HFEA workbook lists MVA1 under OMIM 257300. Search the [official register](https://www.hfea.gov.uk/pgt-m-conditions/) by condition/OMIM, or use our [offline gene lookup](pgtm-navigator.html) as a starting point. Condition approval is distinct from test feasibility, eligibility, funding and likelihood of success. **[OUR MEASUREMENT — workbook 5 August 2026]**

Questions to bring:

- What needs to be confirmed before a reliable reproductive test can be designed?
- Which options are available to us, and what are their limitations?
- What are the clinic's relevant success rates, risks, costs and waiting times?
- Can we obtain counselling before deciding, and return to the decision later?

## 3. Research participation

Our proposed arimoclomol experiment is laboratory research, not an offered treatment or enrolling trial. Ask about any real opportunities through the clinical team. Consent discussions should cover purpose, burden, privacy, return of results, withdrawal and whether benefit is expected. No participation or treatment choice is required by this document.

## 4. What matters most to us

Use these prompts in your own words; there is no score and no “correct” preference.

- The topic we would prefer to leave for later is:
- What we understood the plan to be, and who will confirm it:
